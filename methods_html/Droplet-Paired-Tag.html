Droplet Paired-Tag (10x Genomics)

Droplet Paired-Tag profiles a targeted histone modification and gene expression from the same nucleus using antibody-directed pA-Tn5 tagmentation and the 10x Chromium Next GEM Single Cell Multiome platform. This page covers the 2023 Xie et al. protocol with Chromium X, Chip J and ARC-v1 chemistry.

See original plate-based Paired-Tag for split-pool barcoding, and 10x Multiome for the underlying commercial chemistry. Here, CUT&Tag replaces the initial accessibility tagmentation; the DNA signal reports the antibody target. The H3K27ac and H3K27me3 experiments are separate assays, each paired with RNA.

Adapter and primer sequences

All sequences are 5' to 3' unless an antiparallel strand is explicitly labelled. /5Phos/ denotes a 5' phosphate and /3ddC/ a 3' dideoxycytidine. The four custom oligos below are transcribed exactly from Supplementary Table 1; download them as CSV.

Custom oligoSequence (5' to 3')Role
AdapterA/5Phos/TCGTCGGCAGCGTCAGATGTGTATAAGAGACAGTransferred A-side adapter; its 5′ phosphate permits ligation to the bead capture oligo.
AdapterBGTCTCGTGGGCTCGGAGATGTGTATAAGAGACAGTransferred B-side adapter; provides the opposite DNA-library handle.
pMENTs-Bridge/5Phos/CTGTCTCTTATACACATCTGACGCTGCCGACGACAGACGCGNon-transferred strand with a 3′ extension that bridges AdapterA to the bead spacer. Also called bridge-pMENTs in the protocol.
pMENTs/5Phos/CTGTCTCTTATACACATCT/3ddC/Short non-transferred mosaic-end strand; 3′ blocked.

The bridge oligo has no 3' ddC annotation in the source table, and AdapterB has no 5' phosphate annotation. Preserve those distinctions when reading the sequences.

Step-by-step library generation

(1) Assemble the two pA-Tn5 adapter preparations

Anneal AdapterA with pMENTs-Bridge, and AdapterB with pMENTs. Load each annealed preparation into pA-Tn5, then mix equal volumes as specified in the authors' protocol. The extended bridge is the custom connection to the Multiome DNA-capture oligo.

(2) Perform antibody-directed tagmentation in nuclei

Pre-couple the histone-mark antibody with the assembled pA-Tn5, incubate with nuclei, wash and activate tagmentation. The transferred AdapterA and AdapterB strands provide the handles required for the subsequent DNA library. Cellular barcodes have not yet been attached.

Productive A/B-ended DNA fragment (schematic, one strand):
5'- [AdapterA handle][ME][genomic insert][rc(ME)][rc(AdapterB handle)] -3'

This diagram follows an amplifiable A/B-ended fragment; it does not imply that every tagmentation product has this end combination. rc means reverse complement.

(3) Encapsulate nuclei with Multiome beads

Load tagmented nuclei with the barcoding mixture into Chip J on Chromium X. In each occupied GEM, the bead supplies DNA-capture oligos and barcoded oligo-dT primers. Droplet identity replaces the multiple split-pool barcode rounds of the original method.

(4) Attach a DNA barcode by bridge-assisted ligation

The bridge strand is reverse-complementary to the complete AdapterA plus the bead's terminal spacer. It aligns the 3' end of the capture oligo next to AdapterA's 5' phosphate. Ligation joins them without copying a UMI onto the DNA molecule.

Antiparallel duplex (| = nick at the phosphorylated 5' end of AdapterA):

5'- [P5][BC-DNA]CGCGTCTG | TCGTCGGCAGCGTCAGATGTGTATAAGAGACAG[genomic DNA] -3'
            3'- GCGCAGAC   AGCAGCCGTCGCAGTCTACACATATTCTCTGTC /5Phos/-5'
                pMENTs-Bridge (3' to 5'; bases aligned above)

After ligation, the barcode-bearing strand is:
5'- [P5][16-base BC-DNA]CGCGTCTGTCGTCGGCAGCGTCAGATGTGTATAAGAGACAG[genomic DNA] -3'

The spacer is 8 bases long and is distinct from the 16-base cell barcode. The bridge is a ligation scaffold; it is not a second cellular barcode.

(5) Reverse-transcribe RNA and template-switch in the same GEM

The RNA capture oligo primes polyadenylated RNA. Reverse transcription incorporates BC-RNA and a 12-base UMI into cDNA; template switching supplies the distal amplification handle. The authors use a modified GEM incubation of 37 °C for 45 minutes, then 25 °C for 30 minutes, followed by quenching.

RNA-derived first-strand cDNA (schematic):
5'- [R1 handle][16-base BC-RNA][12-base UMI][poly(dT)VN][cDNA][template-switch-derived handle] -3'

(6) Recover, pre-amplify and split the products

Break the GEMs, clean up and pre-amplify using the Multiome workflow. The published modification elutes in 80 µl after pre-amplification cleanup, then uses 40 µl for DNA preparation and 17.5 µl plus 17.5 µl water for RNA preparation.

Final library structures

Both diagrams are oriented from P5 to P7, with the top strand written 5' to 3'. Bracketed handles and inserts are schematic. Sample indices identify libraries; the DNA cell barcode occupies the physical i5/Index 2 position.

The DNA Index 2 arrow illustrates the reverse-complement workflow used here; the instrument and demultiplexer determine how that index is represented in FASTQ. The DNA R1/R2 pair covers the biological fragment, whereas RNA R1 is the barcode/UMI read.

DNA Index 2

Raw 24-cycle Index 2 in the reverse-complement workflow:
Cycles:    1......8   9..............24
Sequence: CAGACGCG  [rc(BC-DNA), 16 bases]
           spacer      cell barcode

Coordinates are 1-based, inclusive. The first 8 bases are the reverse complement of the bead spacer CGCGTCTG. Remove or mask the spacer before barcode correction. If conversion already removed the spacer or reverse-complemented the index read, do not apply those transformations again.

The 10x guide (Rev F, pp. 69–71) permits 24-cycle I2 on NextSeq 1000/2000 without a custom recipe. Instruments such as NextSeq 500/550 instead require the specified 8-dark-cycle + 16-read-cycle recipe. In that output, the 16 barcode bases need not have an 8-base FASTQ prefix.

RNA Read 1

Cycles:   1..............16  17.........28
Sequence: [BC-RNA, 16 bases][UMI, 12 bases]

RNA I1 and I2 are sample indices, not cellular barcodes. Apply the instrument-appropriate i5 index orientation when demultiplexing. The original Paired-Tag Read 2 split-pool parser does not describe either of these droplet libraries.

Match DNA and RNA from the same bead

10x documents a one-to-one pairing of different ATAC and GEX barcode sequences. Match the DNA-side barcode to its RNA-side partner using the ARC-v1 translation map, after orientation normalization and error correction. A reverse complement alone is not the DNA-to-RNA barcode translation.

The repository already contains the matching DNA/ATAC whitelist and RNA/GEX whitelist. All 736,320 row pairs were checked against the authors' full barcode translation table. Preserve their order if deriving a map; do not sort the two lists independently.

Download these four examples. Cell Ranger ARC's ATAC CB tag is already corrected and translated; its CR tag is the raw barcode. Avoid translating an already translated barcode. Keep GEM/sample identity when combining experiments.

The authors' separate processing scripts use --chemistry=ARC-v1 with Cell Ranger for RNA and Cell Ranger ATAC for DNA. The DNA branch uses fragment coordinates and cell barcode for duplicate handling; the RNA branch uses its UMI. CUT&Tag peak calling and QC should reflect the histone target even though the library architecture follows Multiome ATAC.
