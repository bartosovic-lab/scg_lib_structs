sciCUT&Tag2in1

sciCUT&Tag2in1 (Janssens et al., 2025, Cell Reports) jointly profiles RNA Polymerase II and a histone modification in the same single cell. It is built on sciCUT&Tag (Janssens et al., 2024, Nature Protocols), which uses protein A-Tn5 (pA-Tn5) loaded with barcoded adapters instead of free Tn5. Cells are tagmented in 96 wells, each with a different pair of Tn5 barcodes. The cells are then pooled and dispensed into the 72 × 72 = 5,184 nanowells of a TaKaRa ICELL8 chip, where PCR adds a second barcode pair (i5/i7). A cell is identified by the combination of the Tn5 barcodes and the ICELL8 i5/i7 indices. In the 2in1 version the cells are tagmented twice in the same well: first for Pol II with sci-pA-Tn5 plate 1, then for the histone mark with sci-pA-Tn5 plate 2. The two plates carry different Tn5 barcodes. The sequences here are taken from Supplementary Table 1 of the Cell Reports paper, which also covers sciCUT&Tag (plate 1 alone).


Is this the same library structure as sci-ATAC-seq?

The final library structure is the same as sci-ATAC-seq, with the same Tn5 adapter backbone from Amini et al., 2014. The differences are in the barcodes, the PCR indices, the sequencing primers and the way the cells are split:

Step-by-step library generation

(1) Anneal barcoded Tn5 sequences s5/s7 to Tn5MErev and load pA-Tn5. Each of the 96 wells of a sci-pA-Tn5 plate gets a unique s5 × s7 pair (8 × 12). Plate 1 uses s5_1..8 × s7_1..12 (Pol II). Plate 2 uses s5_9..16 × s7_13..24 (histone mark, barcodes 97-192):

The transposome is the same as in sci-ATAC-seq, apart from being fused to protein A.

(2) Bind lightly fixed whole cells to ConA beads, incubate with Pol II antibodies (Ser5P, Ser2P, Ser2/5P) and guinea pig anti-rabbit secondary, split into 96 wells, and tagment with sci-pA-Tn5 plate 1 under CUTAC conditions:

This gives the same three product types as sci-ATAC-seq: s5/s5, s7/s7 and s5/s7. Only s5/s7 fragments are amplified efficiently (semi-suppressive PCR). Here is the amplifiable product:

(3) In the same wells, block with rabbit Fc peptide, incubate with the histone antibody (H3K4me1-2-3 mix in 48 wells, H3K27me3 in 48 wells) and secondary, and tagment again with sci-pA-Tn5 plate 2 under CUT&Tag conditions:

The second round produces fragments with the same structure, but with plate-2 barcodes. Because each cell stays in the same well, well k of plate 1 and well k of plate 2 mark the same cells. Fragments are grouped by where their two Tn5 barcodes come from:

For pairing, the authors rewrote the histone and split-read barcodes to the plate-1 (Pol II) barcodes of the same cell. The plate-1 to plate-2 well map is not given in the paper, so it has to come from the plate layout used.

(4) Pool, filter, DAPI stain and dispense cells into an ICELL8 350v chip (5,184 nanowells). Add SDS + thermolabile proteinase K (37 °C 1 h, 58 °C 1 h), then Triton X-100, then one sci_Ad1 (i5) primer per chip row and one sci_Ad2 (i7) primer per chip column, then KAPA HiFi mix. This is what a reverse-complement (Workflow B) instrument such as the NextSeq 2000 produces. On a forward-strand instrument the whole read is reverse complemented: i5, then s5, then the Tn5 s5 barcode.
Read 279gDNA

The authors demultiplex with sciCTextract. It takes Index 1 positions 1-8 as the Tn5 s7 barcode and 36-43 as i7. It then reverse complements Index 2 (unless --forward-mode is set) and takes positions 1-8 as i5 and 30-37 as the Tn5 s5 barcode. It allows one mismatch per barcode. In its barcode tables, s5 and i5 are given as in the oligos, and s7 and i7 as reverse complements of the oligos. The cell barcodes in the authors' output files are the 32-nt concatenation i7 + i5 + Tn5 s7 + Tn5 s5.
